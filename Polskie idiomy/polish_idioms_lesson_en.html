The Polish Idioms Directory

Dive into eight classic, colorfully rich expressions, completely parsed for modern learners.

Easy Everyday
Robić z igły widły
Literal: To make pitchforks out of a needle
Real Meaning: To exaggerate or make a mountain out of a molehill

Used when someone overreacts, blowing small complications completely out of proportion.

Example: Nie panikuj tak, robisz z igły widły. Don't panic so much, you are exaggerating.
Medium Humor
Myśleć o niebieskich migdałach
Literal: To think about blue almonds
Real Meaning: To daydream or be absent-minded

Perfect when pointing out someone who is totally lost in pleasant daydreaming instead of working.

Example: Zamiast słuchać nauczyciela, ona znowu myśli o niebieskich migdałach. Instead of listening to the teacher, she is daydreaming again.
Easy Emotion
Czuć miętę do kogoś
Literal: To feel mint towards someone
Real Meaning: To have a crush on or be attracted to someone

Refers to romantic chemistry or finding someone exceptionally appealing.

Example: Od razu poczułem do niej miętę. I immediately felt a crush on her.
Medium Warning
Obiecywać gruszki na wierzbie
Literal: To promise pears on a willow tree
Real Meaning: To make unrealistic or impossible promises

Used to call out someone promising magical results that cannot possibly happen in reality.

Example: Oni obiecują gruszki na wierzbie, nie wierz im. They are making empty promises, don't believe them.
Easy Work / Study
Rzucić okiem
Literal: To throw an eye
Real Meaning: To take a quick look or glance at something

Extremely useful in business contexts when you want someone to review something quickly.

Example: Czy możesz rzucić okiem na tę prezentację? Could you take a quick look at this presentation?
Easy Conversation
Mówić prosto z mostu
Literal: To speak straight from the bridge
Real Meaning: To speak frankly / get straight to the point

Used when encouraging honesty, getting straight to the facts without beating around the bush.

Example: Powiem ci prosto z mostu: to nie zadziała. I'll tell you straight out: this won't work.
Advanced Humor
Urwać się z choinki
Literal: To fall/break off a Christmas tree
Real Meaning: To be completely clueless or out of touch with reality

A funny, slightly sarcastic way to tell someone they act like they came from another planet.

Example: Zachowujesz się, jakbyś urwał się z choinki! You act as if you've just dropped from another planet!
Advanced Everyday
Wiercić komuś dziurę w brzuchu
Literal: To drill a hole in someone's stomach
Real Meaning: To pester or persistently nag someone

Describes constant pleading or reminding until you finally yield or give in.

Example: On wierci mi dziurę w brzuchu o ten wyjazd. He keeps nagging me constantly about this trip.

How Polish Idioms Work

Idioms are the heartbeat of the Polish language. Translating them literally will lead to funny confusion!

No Literal Meanings

Polish idioms carry rich historical backgrounds. Thinking of "blue almonds" literally misses the beautiful concept of peaceful daydreaming.

Context is Key

Some idioms match professional spaces (like "rzucić okiem"), while others belong strictly to family or informal humor (like "urwać się z choinki").

Grammar Check

Unlike English idioms, verbs in Polish idioms must still conjugate to fit the subject, tense, and aspect. Pay attention to verb endings!

Used when someone is wildly out of touch with facts everyone knows.

Moje dziecko cały dzień wierci mi dziurę w brzuchu o nową zabawkę.
My child has been nagging me all day about a new toy.
Highlights persistent, exhaustive pestering.
Czuć miętę
Chyba poczułam miętę do nowego kolegi z uniwersytetu.
I think I developed a crush on the new classmate from university.
A warm, cute idiom ideal for describing attraction.
Myśleć o niebieskich migdałach
Przestań wreszcie myśleć o niebieskich migdałach i bierz się do pracy!
Stop daydreaming at last and get down to work!
Common advice given to daydreamers who are neglecting tasks.
Rzucić okiem
Szefie, czy może Pan rzucić okiem na ten raport finansowy?
Boss, could you take a quick look at this financial report?
Perfect for soft, polite work requests.
Mówić prosto z mostu
W biznesie najbardziej cenię ludzi, którzy mówią prosto z mostu.
In business, I value people most who speak frankly and directly.
Highly valued attribute in clear professional communications.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

See where language learners make mistakes and how to construct these phrases correctly.

Unnatural/Wrong Translation
Correct Polish Idiom
Why / Nuance
"Robić góry z igły"
"Robić z igły widły"
Translating the English idiom "mountain out of a molehill" word-for-word fails. Poles make pitchforks (widły) out of needles (igła).
"Czuć miętę dla kogoś"
"Czuć miętę do kogoś"
Use the correct preposition. "Do kogoś" (towards someone) is correct, while "dla kogoś" (for someone) sounds highly unnatural here.
"Rzucić oko"
"Rzucić okiem"
You must use the instrumental case "okiem" (with an eye) rather than the accusative case "oko".

Micro Dialogues in Action

Study quick exchanges demonstrating how these idioms show up in normal everyday chats.

At the Office
Marek
Tomasz, możesz rzucić okiem na ten raport zanim go wyślę?
Tomasz
Jasne! Powiem ci prosto z mostu, jeśli coś będzie do poprawy.
Translation:
Marek: Tomasz, can you take a quick look at this report before I send it?
Tomasz: Sure! I'll tell you straight out if there's anything to fix.
The Classroom
Kasia
Ania znowu patrzy przez okno i myśli o niebieskich migdałach.
Piotr
Chyba poczuła miętę do tego nowego chłopaka!
Translation:
Kasia: Ania is looking out the window again and daydreaming.
Piotr: I think she developed a crush on that new guy!
